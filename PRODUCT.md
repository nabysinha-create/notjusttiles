# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Affluent homeowners furnishing luxury properties, architects, interior designers, and premium furniture shoppers who are specifying or purchasing for high-end residential spaces. The primary visitor is a design-literate buyer who already owns beautiful things and is evaluating whether this brand belongs among them; a secondary visitor is a trade professional (architect or designer) scouting pieces to recommend to a client. Both arrive with high visual literacy and low tolerance for anything that reads as mass-market.

## Product Purpose

NOTJUSTTILES is a curated American furniture brand. The homepage is the first and most important impression of the brand: it must communicate, within seconds of the video loading, that this is a house of taste and craftsmanship rather than a retailer. Success is a visitor who scrolls the full editorial journey, then either explores a collection or books a consultation — not a visitor who bounces because the site reads as a template.

## Positioning

Premium American furniture curated for modern luxury homes. The brand's edge is curation, not manufacturing: every piece has already been selected for taste, craftsmanship, comfort, and timelessness, so the buyer is trusting an eye, not browsing a catalog.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Explore Collection. Secondary CTA: Book Consultation — the lower-commitment path for visitors not ready to shop.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "Curated. Not Collected."
- Belief ladder: this brand has an eye most retailers don't → what it sells is museum-grade in craftsmanship and material → a home built around these pieces will feel timeless, not trend-driven → I should either explore the collection myself or book a consultation to have it done for me.
- Proof on hand: none yet. The Client Testimonials section ships with placeholder-quality quotes, clearly structured so real testimonials, press mentions, or partner/designer logos can be dropped in later without touching layout.

## Brand Personality

Quiet luxury, museum quality, editorial, architectural, timeless, minimal, sophisticated. Named references: Apple (restraint and product theater), Minotti, Poliform, and B&B Italia (Italian furniture-house authority), RH and Studio McGee (editorial American luxury retail), Audo Copenhagen (Scandinavian material honesty), Bocci (object-as-art presentation). The brand should feel confident enough to say very little — one held shot, one line of copy, one piece of furniture — and let the craftsmanship carry the moment.

## Anti-references

Generic Shopify layouts. Template appearance. Heavy gradients. Startup SaaS styling. Bright colors. Playful UI. Large colored buttons. Clutter. Any product presentation that resembles Amazon or Shopify cards. Cheap ecommerce feeling of any kind.

## Design Principles

- **Restraint is the luxury signal.** Whitespace and silence communicate value more than any added element; when in doubt, remove rather than add.
- **Photography and film lead, UI recedes.** Chrome — buttons, cards, nav — never competes with product, material, or interior imagery for attention.
- **Motion narrates, never decorates.** Every animation earns its place by revealing scale, depth, or craftsmanship; nothing moves just to prove the site is alive.
- **Curation over catalog.** Pieces are presented as gallery objects with room to breathe, never as inventory rows.
- **One confident CTA per moment.** Never stack competing calls to action in the same view.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Given the motion-forward direction, every animation (fades, parallax, scroll-driven reveals) ships with a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative that swaps to an instant or simple crossfade state rather than removing the content.
